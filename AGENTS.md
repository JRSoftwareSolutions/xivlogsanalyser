# Log analysis

The dashboard is `dashboard.html` at the repo root. It lists every saved log as a session, grouped by day and time. `<report>/session.html` is that one session. Both are generated. Do not hand-edit them.

A session's day and clock come from `<report>/session.json` (`started`, `title`, `owner`). `started` is the report's time zero. When the user corrects when a log was played, update that file and reanalyze.

Mechanic knowledge for Dragonsong's Reprise lives in `fights/dsr/mechanics.json`. Use it to say what happened, what the mechanic should have been, and whose fault the death was. `notes/classification.md` explains the Thordan calls.

A phase's reviewed mechanics are the `clusters` in that file, drawn top to bottom by `starts`. `skill` on a cluster is the skill for that stop. `debuffs` on a cluster are the role debuffs, such as Dive from Grace's number and dive marker, listed for every player on that stop's pull card. Each one is drawn as its game icon, `src/xivloganalyzer/status_icons/<guid>.png`, so a new debuff needs its icon there. Do not hardcode the order in the page. `markers` in `fights/dsr/fight.json` are pull-chart labels for moments with no death review. They are not stops on that line.

When the user corrects a call ("X should be Y", "Z is not a mistake", "that one is actually a fail"), first record it: `python -m xivloganalyzer confirm <code> --pull N --name "<player>" --outcome <outcome> --owners "A,B" --why "<their words>"` (add `--time M:SS` when the player died twice). That writes `reviews/<code>/verified.json` and keeps what the judge said before as `first_pass`. Then update `fights/dsr/mechanics.json` to match, run `python -m xivloganalyzer reanalyze` so every saved report is judged again, and run `python -m xivloganalyzer verify`. `verify` fails when a call the user made no longer holds. A call an agent checked from the evidence is `--by review`; it is reported, and fails only with `--strict`. `owners` leaves out Party mitigation, Earlier damage, and Earlier mistake. Update `tests/test_reference.py` only when the correction is meant to change the settled Thordan counts.

Each report folder has `calls.tsv`: one line per death with its outcome, cause, owners, `basis` (what decided the owner: their own hit, a debuff, positions, who the cast hit, a role rule, no packet, or a label), and `missing` (inputs the call was made without). A death is keyed by pull, the log's clock time, and the player. `reanalyze` prints the calls it changed. `python -m xivloganalyzer changes [<code>] --since <rev>` lists every call that differs from a git revision. Read that list before committing a rule change; a call you did not mean to move is a bug.

Heavenly Heel is the off tank's: `assignments.off_tank` in `<report>/session.json`, or else the tank it landed on in the most pulls. When the off tank was dead and it hit the main tank, the dead off tank owns it. A Skyward Leap death on the real marker is not raw: short of full HP is the healers, and full HP is missing mitigation. Pull 68 has no raw death. Kitana Kahn's Skyward Leap was full HP with no mitigation. Eternal Conviction is always an empty tower and is a fail. Before 100 seconds it is the Strength towers, Conviction `25567`, soaked by healers and DPS. After 100 seconds it is the Sanctity towers. Holy Impact is comets too close and is a fail; whoever dropped those two comets owns it. A player already dead, alive and outside the tower soak, or sharing a tower owns an empty tower, not the players in their towers (`needs_everyone` in `fights/dsr/mechanics.json`). With no hit list for the soak and nobody dead, it is Missed soak, and the judgment lists the gap in `missing`. A player missing because they were dead passes it on to whoever owned that death. Each missing player is one share, split the way their own death was, so a gap a player left themselves weighs as much as one passed on through someone else. A dead healer's share of a raw death passes on the same way. Sacred Sever jumps alternate between two groups (`alternating`): a jump that landed on the wrong group belongs to the right group's dead players, and a short jump names who of that group was missing. Dragon's Rage is shared by everyone without a Skyward Leap marker, both tanks included; a short stack names who was not in it. Sacred Sever hits harder the shorter Ser Zephirin's jump, so a big hit on a full stack is the group standing too close, each player's own fail. Personal mitigation is what the tank put on themselves; a co-tank's or healer's cooldown counts only through the multiplier. These three come from the logs and the guides (`notes/classification.md`). A stack's role cap is for a full stack and scales with the stack size, so the same cast split fewer ways is a short stack, not a cleave. A player just raised (Transcendent) counts as dead for a stack or tower. A stack is a share or a cleave once per cast: every death in one packet gets the verdict of the middle victim's hit against their role cap. A raw or low death right after the player lived their own gaze, dodge, puddle, or orb hit is theirs when that hit was what they were short. Who broke a mechanic is decided the one way in the `assign-fault` skill, and `python -m xivloganalyzer audit <code>` lists the calls still on weak evidence. With no ability file for a hit and no replay row for it, its multiplier is unknown: the call adds no Party mitigation share and does not count it as missing personal mitigation. A failed Hiemal Storm soak is a fail. Its circles are one support and one DPS (`pairs`): an ice with no partner belongs to the partners who were dead, in no circle, or doubled up in another. Heavy Impact ids 25560 and 25559 are both fails. Bright Flare is a fail.

A death with no damage packet is the deathwall. A damage-over-time kill such as Frostbite links a status instead of an action, and is not the deathwall. Walking into it is a mistake, except when three or more players walk in together within three seconds as the pull's first event: that is an agreed reset after something the log does not show, such as a bad opener or something at someone's desk. It is nobody's mistake (outcome `environment`, cause `reset`), and a later death it left short passes on to "Group reset". Otherwise, before any other death, Damage Down, or Hysteria in the pull it is that player's alone. After one, it is still a fail, shared with "Earlier mistake", unless every earlier death and debuff in the pull was the walker's own. The first mistake of a pull matters most, and later deaths often cascade from it. Each judgment carries `first` and `first_mistake`. Which debuffs count is `cascade_debuffs` in `fights/dsr/fight.json`. The deathwall text is `deathwall` in `fights/dsr/mechanics.json`.

Each report folder has an `analysis.json` stamp: fingerprints of the fight pack (`fights/dsr/`), the analyzer code, and that log's files, from the run that wrote its pages. `reanalyze` judges again only the reports whose stamp no longer matches, and says why. `reanalyze --all` judges every report. `python -m xivloganalyzer status` lists what is out of date without writing anything. Commit the stamps, `calls.tsv`, and `inputs.json` with the pages. Do not hand-edit them.

The intake for a new log, step by step, is the `review-new-log` skill. A new log goes in `reports/<code>/` with `fights.json`, `deaths-html.json`, and ability files, then `python -m xivloganalyzer analyze <code>`. `analyze` writes `inputs.json` and warns when an input is missing. Run `python -m xivloganalyzer check <code>`, then `python -m xivloganalyzer fetch <code>` to get the ability files it lists from the FFLogs API (FFLOGS_CLIENT_ID and FFLOGS_CLIENT_SECRET), before trusting the calls. Do not script around the site's human check; the site's pages need a browser session a person clicks through.

Run these from the repo root. When the package is not installed (`pip install -e .`), prefix them with `PYTHONPATH=src`. The tests are `python -m unittest discover -s tests`.

To overview a session without loading full JSON, use `review-session` and `python -m xivloganalyzer brief <code>` (optional `--pull`, `--mechanic`, `--outcome`, `--write`). The brief opens with what started each pull, who started pulls, and who owns the night's deaths; each judgment's `first_causes` holds its pull's first mistake per mechanic with its owners. To check one call against the log, `python -m xivloganalyzer evidence <code> --pull N` prints every hit, marker, debuff, and death of the pull in time order, then the judge's calls. `--blind` leaves the calls out, for a review that should not see them first.
